# Pricing Strategy

## Read first
- `projects/<project-slug>/strategy/intake.md`: customer, stage, current price and
  paying customers, sales motion.
- The competitor, ICP and positioning memos and the evidence log when present.
- Missing facts that change the answer (current price, number of paying customers,
  who signs the check): ask once, then go on with stated assumptions.

## Method
1. **Find the value.** What the customer gains or saves per month in their terms
   (hours, lost bookings, a hire avoided). Put a rough number on it and show the math.
2. **Find the range.** Competitor and workaround prices, with sources and dates. The
   status quo has a price too (staff time, errors, a spreadsheet's upkeep).
3. **Pick the value metric:** what the price scales with (seats, locations,
   bookings, usage). It should grow as the customer gets more value.
4. **Build 2 or 3 model options:** one-time, subscription, usage-based, freemium,
   free plus paid services. For each, the trade-off for this stage and motion.
5. **Set a number per option** from value and range, not cost. Founders usually
   underprice: say so when the evidence points that way.
6. **Packaging:** at most 3 tiers, one marked as the one most people should pick,
   each tier separated by a feature that matters to a segment, not by limits alone.
7. **Existing customers:** for a price change, say how current customers move
   (kept at their price for a period, or moved with notice) and the risk.

## Options
A short table: model, price, value metric, who it suits, main risk. Freemium is a
growth plan with real costs (support, free users who never pay), not a safety net.

## Recommendation
One option with the price, the reasoning, and the evidence that would change it.
Before validation, pair it with a **price test**: a presale, a quote at the target
price on the next calls, or a paid pilot. One paying customer at the target price is
stronger evidence than a hundred free signups.

## Before you reply
- Every number shows its source or its math; guesses are marked.
- The recommended price is anchored to value, with competitors as a sanity check.
- The plan says what happens to current customers when a price changes.
- The test has a pass line set before it runs.
