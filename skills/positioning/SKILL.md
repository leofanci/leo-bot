# Positioning

## Read first
- `projects/<project-slug>/strategy/intake.md`: customer, problem, offer, evidence.
- The ICP and competitor memos, the assessment and any customer quotes in the
  evidence log. Customer words beat clever words.
- Missing ICP: state the customer you assume in one line and flag it.

## Method
1. **Best-fit customer:** the segment that gets the most value fastest (from the
   ICP memo). One line.
2. **Real alternative:** what that customer would use if this did not exist. Often
   a spreadsheet, a person or doing nothing.
3. **Unique strengths:** what this offer does that the alternative does not. Only
   strengths with proof (a feature, a result, a quote).
4. **Value:** what each strength gets the customer, in their terms (time, money,
   fewer no-shows).
5. **Category frame options** (see Options).
6. **Differentiator per option:** the one claim the rivals cannot honestly make.
   Flag any claim the evidence log cannot back.

## Options
Two or three category frames:
- **Inside an existing category:** buyers already know it; you must beat the leaders
  on one point.
- **Subcategory:** the known category plus a qualifier ("for small clinics").
  Familiar and ownable.
- **New category:** room to lead, but expensive to teach; only with budget and a
  clear new behavior.
For each: the one-liner it implies and the main risk.

## Recommendation
One frame, then the message for it:
- One-liner: 15 words or fewer, in the customer's words.
- Pitch: three sentences: problem, what this does, proof or why now.
- Three pillars, each with a proof point from the evidence log, or marked as
  needing proof.
- Words to avoid: jargon the customer would not use.

Then two quick tests: two one-liners on a landing page or bio, or read aloud on the
next five calls, with what result picks the winner.

## Before you reply
- The alternative is named and is real for this customer.
- Every pillar has proof or is marked as unproven.
- The one-liner reads like the customer talking, not a slogan.
- No superlatives without proof ("best", "leading", "revolutionary").
