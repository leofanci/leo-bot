# Objection Handling

## Read first
- `projects/<project-slug>/strategy/intake.md`, the positioning, pricing and
  competitor memos.
- The evidence log: objections already heard and what worked.
- Missing: ask for the exact words the buyer used and at what stage.

## Method
1. **Write the objection in the buyer's words.**
2. **Find what it may mean.** "Too expensive" can mean no budget, not enough value
   shown, or a cheaper rival. List the 2 or 3 likely meanings.
3. **A question back for each meaning**, to learn which it is ("Compared with what?",
   "What would it need to do to be worth it?").
4. **An answer per meaning:** short, in the customer's words, with one proof (a
   number, a customer quote, a trial). No pressure tactics, no fake urgency.
5. **Know when to stop:** objections that mean "not a fit" (wrong size, no problem)
   end the deal politely and are logged as learning.
6. **Common set:** price, timing ("not now"), status quo ("we use paper"), a rival,
   trust ("you are small"), and effort ("no time to switch"). Prepare the ones this
   offer meets most.

## Options
For a repeated objection, two or three fixes beyond the script: change the offer
(trial, pilot, monthly), change the proof (case study, guarantee), or change the
segment. Trade-offs in one line each.

## Recommendation
The answer to use for the objection asked about, and the fix if it keeps coming up.

## Before you reply
- Every answer is true and backed by proof or marked as needing it.
- No manipulation: no false scarcity, no made-up deadlines or numbers.
- Replies to a real buyer are drafts that wait for the owner.

## Write gate
Share the answers in chat. Log a repeated objection with `add-evidence` only after the user approves.
